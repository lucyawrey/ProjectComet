# Classes and Skills

One character can become any class. Classes are carried as **Class Crystals**, swapped almost anywhere, and every class's progress is kept forever. Gentle costs encourage players to specialise without ever stopping them from doing everything.

## Class Crystals

A Class Crystal is the only thing a character equips. It's a key that grants the use of one of the character's classes, and it carries everything that goes with that class: a skill loadout, a gear set, an outfit and optionally an appearance change, since becoming a class can transform how the character looks.

- **Swapping crystals** works almost anywhere, even in combat, with an interruptible cast. It changes class, skills, gear and outfit at once. Some areas or encounters can forbid it.
- **Crystals are soulbound.** Others can carry yours and hand it back, but only you can use it.
- **Several crystals can share a class**, for different builds of it.
- **Crystals take inventory space**, so carrying many slowly fills your bags: a gentle push towards a focused set.

### Getting more crystals

Each new crystal for a class you don't already hold costs more Anima, possibly plus materials. The cost depends on what you hold now, never on your history, so letting a crystal go makes the next one cheaper. Crystals from quests are free and don't raise the price. A generous hard cap sits above the number of classes.

## Promotion

Classes form a tree rooted at **Freelancer**, the only tier 1 class, which every character gets in the prologue. Freelancer promotes into tier 2 classes, which promote into high-tier classes.

Promoting converts a crystal into the next class for a small cost. The first time a class is unlocked this way, experience moves from the class you promoted from (the new class needs a lot of experience to reach level 1); promoting into a class you already have costs nothing.

Each part of the game has its own level cap (the early part lower than the late part). Tier 2 classes promote around the early part's cap, and high tiers level to the late cap. Exact numbers come from balancing.

## The Crystal Archives

The Crystal Archives keep every class's experience and highest level permanently. No progress is ever lost, even if a crystal is.

## Experience, Soul XP and Anima

- **Experience** goes to the equipped class. Anything earned past a maxed class or craft becomes **Soul XP**, so no experience is wasted. An unlockable toggle can send experience to Soul XP directly.
- **Soul XP** is a currency for permanent unlocks: promotions, Anima Capacity and other lasting improvements. Everything bought with it is permanent.
- **Anima** is the magic of your soul: an energy that refills over time (offline too, up to capacity), and comes from rare items and quests. It is never sold for real money. **Anima Capacity** is how much you can hold.

Anima gathers several gates MMOs already have into one resource, to encourage particular ways of playing:

- **Teleports**, so travelling on foot stays worthwhile. An emergency teleport to safety works even at zero Anima.
- **New crystals**, to encourage specialisation.
- **Entry to certain content**, such as high-end raids that shouldn't be cleared too often. It's charged on entry and refunded if you don't clear; clears give loot reliably. Party members without enough Anima are warned before anyone is charged.
- **Magical appearance changes**, and perhaps crafting certain endgame items.

## Skills

Actions come in two tiers:

- **Skills** are primary actions, used in normal combat, with proper default controls on keyboard and controller. About ten slots in total.
- **Abilities** are everything else: long-term buffs, teleports, transformations. Less frequent, often useful outside combat, and optionally placed on hotbars.

Each class has a designer-made **core kit** of Skills plus a few **flex slots**. Flex slots take optional Skills from the class or Skills from other classes you hold a crystal for. Jump, crouch, dodge and sprint have dedicated controls but are class skills too: a dragoon-style class might jump higher, a rogue might sneak faster when crouching.

Skills are learned from levelling and from class tutors, plus some special tutors, items and quests.

## Runes

**Runes** are slotted skills: passives, modifiers to Skills, and grants of extra Abilities. They go in coloured slots, and a class's slot layout says a lot about it:

- **Offense** and **Support** for battle (hybrid classes show both);
- **World** for everything outside battle, including crafting, so a class's World slots decide how good a crafter it is.

Primary Skills are shown the same way, as their own colour of slot tied to a control, with core slots shown as locked to the class. The concepts stay distinct; the interface just reads as one system.

- **Rune stones** are Runes as tradeable items. Your first copy of a rare rune stone is soulbound to you; extras can be traded, broken down or sold.
- **Gear Runes:** gear can carry Runes that are active while worn, for free. Wearing gear builds mastery, and a well-worn piece can have its Rune **extracted** into a rune stone without losing it.
- **Variant Runes** swap a core Skill for a designed alternative. Rare **Unbound Runes** unlock a core slot entirely, so any allowed Skill can go there.

## Outfit Magic

An outfit is a real piece of gear worn over your equipment, not a copied look. Outfits always supply the character's **World** Runes, so world utility (like resisting cold) always matches what the character visibly wears. Battle stats come from the gear underneath. Any gear can be worn as equipment or as an outfit, so an outclassed piece can live on as an outfit.
