# Combat

Combat in Anima is action combat that's extremely approachable: a little like an action RPG, a little like a tab-target MMO. It's simple to understand and hard to master. The rules underneath (hitboxes, height zones, frame data) are Comet's; see [Combat Core](../Comet/Combat%20Core.md).

## Feel

- **Real hitboxes, forgiving timing.** Attacks have shapes in the world, but the game is generous about latency: a dodge you started on your screen counts.
- **An optional lock-on** for players who prefer it.
- **Most attacks don't move anyone**, keeping fights readable; a limited set of skills dash, leap or knock back.
- **Everyone can dodge**, jump and crouch, and height matters: jump over a sweep, crouch under an overhead swing. Some attacks pierce ordinary dodges.

## Bosses and raids

Boss fights are where mastery shows. Mechanics draw on FFXIV and Rabbit and Steel: mostly telegraphed swings and area attacks, with occasional moderate projectile patterns in raids. Raids take up to about 30 players; open-world bosses aim for about 100.

Some high-end content costs Anima to enter, charged on entry and refunded if you don't clear. Clears reward loot reliably rather than by chance. There are no weekly lockouts; the Anima cost and gathering a party are the only limits on reruns. Open-world boss rewards are limited to once per player per spawn cycle.

## Changing class mid-fight

Swapping Class Crystals in combat is allowed, with an interruptible cast, and changes class, skills and gear together. Cooldowns don't reset on a swap.

## Difficulty

Any battle that every player must clear to progress (most main-story fights) can scale down, with an easier setting or NPC help. Dungeons, raids and hard bosses keep one difficulty, so their challenge and rewards stay meaningful.
