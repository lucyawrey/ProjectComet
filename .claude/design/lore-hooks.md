# Lore hooks

**Layer: game (Project Anima).**

Agent-distilled list of what gameplay decisions and the project lead's notes already imply about the setting (2026-10-04). **Raw material for the project lead's own lore writing, not lore.** Nothing here is decided as fiction; each line points at where it came from.

## Souls

- Soul Experience grows a character's soul "in size and power" (`classes.md`). XP that a maxed class or craft can't use flows into the soul.
- Anima is "the magic of your soul": it regenerates over time, has a capacity that can grow, and is spent on teleports, new crystals and entering certain content ("animated as using magic to enter") (`classes.md`, `unlockables.md`).
- Soulbound things are tied to a soul, not just an owner: they can be carried by others but always find their way back (recalled at will, or returning on their own after 24 hours) (`items.md`).
- Only a soul's owner can do anything destructive or transforming to a soulbound item (`items.md`).
- Companions that are released are "set free", not destroyed; a released soulbound companion can still be reclaimed (`companions.md`).
- Everything bought with Soul XP is permanent and part of the character (`classes.md`).
- A line in the project lead's Conversations note: a soul as "a form of inherent meaning", no more absurd arising from a machine than from meat.

## Crystals

- A Class Crystal is how a character becomes a class; the old notes describe shattering it to transform (`notes/+ Quick Notes.md`).
- Crystals can change a character's base appearance, emphasising the transformation (`classes.md`).
- Crystals resonate: several crystals share the same physical gear piece (`items.md`). Gear is presented as being "in" the crystal.
- The old notes call a Class Crystal "a sort of super technology" that merges gear, outfit, appearance and skill crystals (`notes/+ Quick Notes.md`).
- The Crystal Archives remember every class a character has ever had, so progress is never truly lost (`classes.md`).
- Raw crystal is mined, and making a new crystal costs Anima and gets harder the more classes you hold (`classes.md`).
- Freelancer is the root of all classes: no kit of its own, able to draw on everything (`classes.md`).
- Crystals can be granted (by quests, at the start) or bought (`classes.md`).

## Leylines and travel

- Teleporting sends the player's soul along the leylines across the world map; it waits, pulsing, at the destination until it can arrive (`world.md`).
- Teleport destinations must be attuned first, through exploration and quests; flight needs a per-region Flight Attunement (`unlockables.md`).
- All warping is explicitly teleportation magic; travel NPCs act as "narrow" connections between zones (`notes/+ Quick Notes.md`).
- Settlements have stable teleport stations; unstable stations exist and must be stabilised by a player before a party can use them (`world.md`).
- An emergency teleport to the nearest safe place works even with no Anima (`classes.md`).
- Boats and airships run fixed routes; story offers convenient rides (e.g. a carriage) where others must walk a long way (`world.md`).

## Realities

- Each channel is a discrete echo of the same area, a specific reality (`world.md`).
- Moving between channels shows a leyline shimmer (`world.md`).
- Constellations name "nearby" realities that players can align with; lore constellations that players can't select name "further away" realities (`world.md`).
- Free players meet an in-world barrier at the border of later parts of the world; its fiction is open (`proposal.md`).

## Runes and magic in gear

- Runes are skills that can be slotted, carried as rune stones and traded (`skills.md`).
- Gear carries Runes; wearing it long enough builds mastery, and a Rune can then be extracted without harming the gear (`skills.md`).
- Outfit Magic: wearing real gear over the top lets its World Runes replace the gear's, so a character's visible clothes always show their world magic (`skills.md`, `art.md`).

## People and places

- Ancestries differ a lot but share a humanoid body; some have tails, long ears, wings or horns (`art.md`). The old schema had two ancestries: Cat and Human (`archive/dotnet-datacenter`).
- Each character starts in a location suited to their class and ancestry, and the early game follows quest chains tied to that place and class; unlocking a new base class should feel somewhat like playing again (`notes/+ Quick Notes.md`).
- Class tutors teach Skills and should be hard to miss (`skills.md`).
- Settlements hold Storage, stables, teleport stations and possibly recall stations (`items.md`, `companions.md`).
- Names already used: regions *Comet* (dropped 2026-10-04: Comet is now the base's name) and *Crystal*; zones *The City of Crystals* and *Silent Shore*; dungeons *Jail of the Lost* and *Corrupted Castle* (`notes/+ Quick Notes.md`).
- Ari: a player NPC, "one of the other players" (`notes/Story Snippets.md`).
- A world with a bright storybook baseline and some moody, eerie or harsh zones (`art.md`).

## Questions the hooks raise

- Is everyone's soul like the player's, or are players special (soul-wanderers, crystal-bearers, travellers between realities)?
- Who made Class Crystals, and why can only their soul's owner use them?
- What are leylines, and why does travelling them cost a soul's magic?
- Why do realities echo each other, and what makes some "nearer" than others?
- What is the barrier at the edge of later parts of the world?
- Why is Anima scarce enough to ration travel and crystals?
- What does "one of the other players" mean for Ari: do NPCs know about players?
